# Attention-based MIL vs the production linear probe

> Generated by `python -m eval.attention_comparison` from `data/predictions.csv` and `artifacts/eval/model_choice.json`.

ABMIL (gated attention, Ilse et al. 2018; hidden 128, attention 64, dropout 0.25, 40 epochs) was trained on the tile embeddings of `uni2h512_lr` (encoder uni2h, set bach512).

**Held fixed:** training images (218) and labels; patient-grouped CV folds (5 x 3 repeats, seed 44); fold loss: held-out log-loss after temperature fit on the other folds; test split (132 images), scored once; tile embeddings (uni2h512_lr).

**Different:** pooling: learned gated attention over tiles vs the mean of tiles; capacity: a small neural network trained by gradient descent vs L2 logistic regression.

## Cross-validation inside the training split (model selection)

|---|---|---|---|
| abmil | 0.183 ± 0.051 | 0.946 | 1.000 |
| uni2h512_lr (production) | 0.178 ± 0.059 | 0.955 | 1.000 |

Selection rule: one_se: simplest (settings risk_model.complexity, then smaller C) within 1 SE of the best. ABMIL was within one standard error of the best CV loss, so the rule chose the simpler model; ABMIL was not rejected for scoring worse on CV.
